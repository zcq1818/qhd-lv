Beidaihe

The classic seaside resort: long sandy beaches, sunrise at Pigeon Nest, and a relaxed holiday town.

Beach & Coast

Beidaihe Coastal Scenic Area北戴河海滨风景区

Beidaihe is a coastal resort on the northern shore of Bohai Bay in Qinhuangdao, Hebei, first opened as a summer retreat by the Qing government in 1898 and later favored by national leaders for its cooler climate. Its roughly 20-kilometer beach has fine sand and calm, shallow water, making it one of northern China's best natural sea-bathing areas. Highlights include sunrise at Pigeon Nest Park, sunset at Tiger Stone Park, hilltop sea views from Lianfeng Mountain, and the historic villa district. It suits families, swimmers, and history or architecture enthusiasts, especially June through September.

Pigeon Nest Park鸽子窝公园

Pigeon Nest Park sits at the northeastern tip of Beidaihe beach and is the classic spot for watching sunrise over the Bohai Sea, ranked first among Beidaihe's traditional 24 scenic views. It takes its name from wild pigeons that once nested on its rocks. Mao Zedong wrote a famous poem here in 1954, commemorated by a stone tablet. The park's landmark is Eagle Rock, an eagle-shaped outcrop about 17 meters tall. Wooden boardwalks lead through wetlands good for birdwatching and beachcombing at low tide. It suits photographers, families, and early risers.

Tiger Stone Sea Park老虎石海上公园

Tiger Stone Sea Park sits on Beidaihe's central beach, named for a cluster of rocks resembling crouching tigers at the shoreline. Covering about 33,000 square meters, it is free to enter and one of Beidaihe's most popular beaches for swimming, with a gentle slope and fine sand. Paid activities include parasailing, windsurfing, and a children's water park. At low tide, visitors can explore the exposed rocks for shells and small crabs. Sunset here, with the tiger-shaped rocks silhouetted against the sea, is one of Beidaihe's favorite photo spots. It suits swimmers, families, and photographers.

Qinhuangdao Wildlife Zoo秦皇岛野生动物园

Qinhuangdao Wildlife Zoo, on Binhai Avenue in Beidaihe, is one of northern China's larger wildlife parks, covering roughly 5,000 mu. It is split into a drive-through predator zone, where visitors view tigers, lions, and bears from their cars, and a walk-through zone where guests can hand-feed giraffes, alpacas, and other gentle animals, plus a bird enclosure and herbivore areas. It's open year-round, with animals generally more active in the morning and in warmer months. The zoo is a popular half-day to full-day family outing in the Beidaihe area.

Lianfeng Mountain Park联峰山公园

Lianfeng Mountain Park, on Lianfeng Road in Beidaihe, is the highest point in the area at about 153 meters, covering roughly 6,068 mu of pine and cypress forest. It is often described as one of China's earliest forest parks. From the hilltop Wanghai Pavilion, visitors get a panoramic view of the Beidaihe coastline. The dense tree cover keeps the park about 5°C cooler than the beach below, making it a popular summer retreat. Other sights include Lotus Rock and Immortal's Cave, plus scattered historic villas. It suits hikers, photographers, and anyone escaping the summer heat, and is known for autumn foliage around October.

Beidaihe Wetland Park北戴河湿地公园

Beidaihe Wetland Park sits on the coast of Beidaihe District, Qinhuangdao, where the Dai and Yang rivers meet the sea, forming tidal flats, reed marshes and shallow ponds. Restoration began in 2011, and its core area was designated a National Wetland Park in December 2015. Located on the East Asian-Australasian Flyway, it hosts over 400 recorded bird species, including one of the few breeding grounds of the endangered Saunders's gull, earning it a reputation as a world-class birdwatching site. Wooden boardwalks wind through the reeds, making it popular for walking, photography and birdwatching, especially during spring and autumn migration.

Biluota Sea Bar Park碧螺塔海上酒吧公园

Strange House Garden怪楼奇园

Strange House Garden (Guailou Qiyuan) stands near Baihua Hill on Lianfeng Road in Beidaihe. It was rebuilt in 1991 based on a whimsical 1930s villa designed by an American landscape scholar who once lived in Beidaihe, with the park's name inscribed by cartoonist Hua Junwu. Covering about 999 square meters, the maze-like building is famous for its many doors and windows, hidden passages, tilted floors, and mirror rooms that create disorienting optical effects, each room built around a different fantastical theme. Because it's entirely indoors, it works well as a rainy-day option and is popular with families and children.

Shanhaiguan

Where the Great Wall meets the sea. A walled Ming-dynasty town with the famous "First Pass Under Heaven".

History & Culture

Shanhaiguan – First Pass Under Heaven山海关·天下第一关

The First Pass Under Heaven (Tianxia Diyi Guan) is the eastern terminus of the Ming-dynasty Great Wall, built in 1381 under general Xu Da. Its gate tower, Zhenyuan Tower, carries a huge horizontal plaque reading "First Pass Under Heaven," each character about one meter square, traditionally attributed to Ming calligrapher Xiao Xian. Visitors can climb the tower for views over the old walled town and the wall itself; the fortified walls, barbican, and nearby Great Wall Museum are well preserved. It is a different ticketed site from the nearby Old Dragon Head (where the wall meets the sea) and from the free-to-enter Shanhaiguan Old Town — don't confuse them.

Old Dragon's Head (Laolongtou)老龙头景区

Old Dragon's Head (Laolongtou) sits on the Bohai Sea coast in Shanhaiguan District, Qinhuangdao, about 5-8 km south of Shanhaiguan's old town. Built in 1381 during the Ming dynasty and later expanded, it marks the easternmost starting point of the Great Wall and is the only stretch where the wall runs into the sea. Destroyed in 1900 and rebuilt in 1984, the site now covers roughly 700 mu and includes the Chenghai Tower viewing platform, the stone sea-wall fortress (Rushi Shicheng), and the Sea God Temple. It suits history and Great Wall enthusiasts and families comfortable with stairs and coastal walking.

Shanhaiguan Old Town山海关古城

Built in 1381 during the Ming dynasty, Shanhaiguan Old Town sits at the eastern end of the Great Wall and was a key military pass, known as the "First Pass Under Heaven." The walled town keeps Ming-and-Qing-era streets and courtyards, with the East Gate watchtower, a bell-and-drum tower, and former military offices. Entry to the town is free; the watchtower and some historic buildings charge separate admission. It suits history and architecture fans, families, and photographers, and pairs well with Old Dragon Head and Jiaoshan Great Wall.

Jiaoshan Great Wall角山长城

Jiaoshan Great Wall sits about 3 km north of Shanhaiguan's old town in Qinhuangdao, Hebei. At roughly 519 m, it is the first mountain the Ming-dynasty Great Wall climbs, earning it the nickname "the first mountain of the Great Wall." The wall follows a steep ridge to the summit, Dapingding, which offers panoramic views over Shanhaiguan and Bohai Bay. Other sights include the historic Hanmen Pass and Qixian Temple. A cable car is available for visitors who prefer not to hike the steep sections. Maple foliage in autumn is a particular highlight. Best suited to travelers who enjoy hiking and Great Wall history; families and older visitors may prefer the cable car.

Yansai Lake (Yansaihu)燕塞湖

Yansai Lake sits in Shanhaiguan District, Qinhuangdao, about 3.5 km northwest of the old Shanhaiguan fortress town. Formed in 1974 when a dam was built across the Shi River, it became the first man-made lake at the eastern end of the Great Wall; the river once served as the fortress's natural defensive barrier and was the site of historical battles from the Sui-Tang era through the 1933 Great Wall resistance against Japan. Known as the "Guilin of the North" for its green hills and clear water, the lake offers boat cruises past unusual rock formations and a bird park with over 100 species. It is especially popular in autumn for colorful foliage.

Mengjiangnu Temple孟姜女庙

Mengjiangnu Temple, also called the Temple of the Faithful Maiden, sits on Fenghuang (Phoenix) Hill about 6 km east of Shanhaiguan's old town. Said to predate the Song dynasty and rebuilt in the Ming era, it commemorates one of China's four great folk legends, in which Meng Jiangnu's weeping is said to have broken a section of the Great Wall. Visitors climb 108 stone steps to the main hall, which holds a painted statue of Meng Jiangnu and a famous punning couplet. Behind the temple, the Wangfu (Longing for Husband) Rock marks where she is said to have watched for her husband. A garden area below retells the legend through staged scenes. Allow 1-2 hours.

Changshoushan Scenic Area (Mountain of Longevity)长寿山景区

Changshoushan (Mountain of Longevity) is a mountain scenic area in Shanhaiguan District, Qinhuangdao, Hebei, about 9 km northeast of the Shanhaiguan old town, backed by the Great Wall. Rated a national 4A attraction, it is themed around China's "longevity" (shou) culture. Its best-known feature is Xuanyang Cave, said to be northern China's largest natural granite cave, with chambers nested within chambers. The site also holds a "forest" of stone tablets carved with the character for longevity by historical calligraphers, a grotto with sculptures honoring famous physicians, and scattered cliff inscriptions. Suited to family visits, older travelers, hikers and photographers; the trails involve many steps.

Wang Family Courtyard (Shanhaiguan Folk Museum)山海关古城民俗博物馆（王家大院）

Wang Family Courtyard, also known as the Shanhaiguan Folk Museum, sits just west of the First Pass Under Heaven watchtower and is billed as the "first house along the Great Wall." Built in the late Ming to early Qing period, it is a classic Ming-Qing courtyard complex of three linked courtyards and 18 exhibition halls on about 2,000+ sq m of floor space. It was originally the home of Wang Sanfo, a wealthy Shanxi salt merchant. Today it houses folk-life exhibits recreating Qing-era domestic scenes, offering visitors a window into Shanhaiguan's old-city history and Shanxi merchant culture.

Laojunding Scenic Area老君顶景区

The arty coast south of the city: Aranya, golden-sand beaches, hot springs and vineyards.

Art & Lifestyle

Aranya Gold Coast Community阿那亚·黄金海岸社区

Aranya is a gated seaside community at Golden Coast in Changli County, Qinhuangdao, known for the "Lonely Library" (Sanlian Seaside Public Library) designed by architect Dong Gong, whose floor-to-ceiling windows face the sea — one of China's first free public libraries by the beach. The community also includes a seaside auditorium, a Dune Art Museum, and a beachfront market, and hosts an annual theatre festival. Access is restricted: non-residents typically need a reservation or to be a paying guest, and the library requires a separate booking via its WeChat account or the Aranya app. It's popular with young creatives, couples, and photographers, especially at sunrise and sunset.

Yutian Qilihai Resort渔田·七里海度假区

Yutian Qilihai Resort sits in the Qilihai area of Beidaihe New District, between Houli and Dahanzhuang villages. Newly rated as a 4A attraction in 2025, it covers about 8,000 mu (roughly 530 hectares) with 5,000 mu of water area. The resort combines large-scale live shows, fishing-culture exhibits, close-up animal encounters (giraffes, capybaras, miniature horses), themed shopping streets, and boutique lodging. Daytime visits focus on animal interaction and water play, while evenings feature a large light-and-water show. It operates seasonally from late April to early October and is best enjoyed as a full-day-into-evening visit.

Nandaihe Gold Coast南戴河·黄金海岸

The Gold Coast, next to the Nandaihe resort area in Changli County, is one of China's best natural sandy coastlines — fine sand, a gentle slope, clear water, and mild tides. Behind the beach lie genuine sand dunes, an unusual dune-meets-sea landscape. Its signature attraction is the International Sand-Sliding Center, one of the country's larger sand-sliding grounds, where visitors ride a board down the dunes toward the sea; jet skiing and banana boats are also available. The open beach itself is free; the sand-sliding center and other activity zones operate seasonally with separate tickets. It suits young travelers, families, and water-sports fans.

Wufeng Mountain Scenic Area五峰山景区

Wufeng (Five Peaks) Mountain lies north of Changli County, about 10 km from Jieshi Mountain. Its five distinctively shaped peaks, the highest around 507 m, form a screen-like ridge long listed among the "Ten Scenes of Ancient Jieshi." It is also historically significant as a site where the early Chinese Marxist Li Dazhao took refuge and wrote key essays. A statue of Li Dazhao, inscribed rocks, and the Ming-dynasty Hanwengong Shrine remain on the mountain, making it a mix of hiking and revolutionary-history tourism.

Jieshi Mountain碣石山

Jieshi Mountain, north of Changli County, rises to 695 meters at its main peak, Xiantai Ding. It is one of the few mountains near the Bohai coast that Chinese emperors historically climbed to worship the sea, most famously Cao Cao, who wrote his poem "Gazing at the Sea from Jieshi" here after a campaign in 207 CE. Qin Shi Huang, Emperor Wu of Han and other rulers also visited. The mountain holds old temple ruins and stone inscriptions, and the summit offers views over the Bohai Sea and Changli's Golden Coast. It suits hikers and history enthusiasts; reaching the top takes about two hours on foot.

Its signature Marselan wine has won 152 international awards, and the estate is open to the public for tours and tastings.

Zushan Scenic Area祖山风景区

Zushan Scenic Area lies in Qinglong Manchu Autonomous County, Qinhuangdao, on the southern slope of the Yan Mountains near the Bohai coast. Its main peak, Tiannv Feng, rises to 1,428 meters and is nicknamed the "Little Huangshan of the north" for its forested peaks and sea of clouds at sunrise. The area is a national scenic and geological park with old-growth forest, azaleas in spring, cool summer air, red foliage in autumn and rime ice in winter. Highlights include Tiannv Feng peak, the Ten-Li Gallery and Gallery Valley. It suits hikers and photographers; visitors with average fitness often take the cable car up and walk down.

Banchangyu Great Wall Scenic Area板厂峪长城景区

Banchangyu Great Wall Scenic Area sits about 29 km from downtown Qinhuangdao in a village that served as a logistics base for Ming-dynasty Great Wall construction. Excavations in 2002 uncovered roughly 600 Ming-era brick kilns used to fire wall bricks — the largest such find in China — along with the world's only known fossil of a hyena species, now displayed on site. A steep, undulating stretch of wall known as the "inverted Great Wall" is a favorite subject for photographers, and the park also has a temple, a cave, a narrow rock passage and waterfalls. Autumn foliage against the old wall is a particular highlight. It suits Great Wall enthusiasts, photographers and visitors interested in Ming-era military history and archaeology.

Bingtangyu Great Wall Canyon冰塘峪大峡谷

Bingtangyu Great Wall Canyon sits at the foot of the Ming Great Wall in Liangjiawan village, Funing District, Qinhuangdao. It is named for patches of ice that reportedly survive in the canyon even during the hottest summer days, an unusual sight for a site at this latitude. Great Wall ruins run along the canyon's ridges, and the site features a roughly 200-meter glass skywalk over the cliffs plus a river-rafting activity. The canyon stays noticeably cooler than the surrounding area in summer, making it a popular local escape from the heat and drawing a growing number of international visitors. It suits adventure seekers, families and anyone looking to cool off in summer; the glass skywalk may not suit those afraid of heights.

Longyungu (Dragon Cloud Valley) Scenic Area龙云谷景区

Longyungu Scenic Area sits in Zhanggezhuang Village, Daxinzhai Township, Funing District, its main peak reaching 905 meters. A national 3A attraction, it features a roughly 3,000-meter wooden boardwalk through the canyon, a 530-meter glass skywalk, and North China's first cliff-side rafting course (~2,200 m), alongside waterfalls such as Longtan and Tianchi. In summer the canyon fills with wildflowers from some 28 species, and winter brings striking ice-waterfall scenery, making it a year-round destination for hikers and photographers.

Tianma Mountain Scenic Area天马山旅游风景区

Tianma ("Heavenly Horse") Mountain sits about 10 km north of Funing District, Qinhuangdao, rising to roughly 300 m. It is named for a summit boulder resembling a galloping horse. Ming-dynasty general Qi Jiguang, who defended this stretch of the Great Wall, left several rock inscriptions here, now a provincial cultural relic. The mountain features stacked boulders, caves, and whimsically shaped rocks such as "Monkey Watching the Sea." It's an easy half-day hike combining natural scenery with Ming border-defense history.

Tianma Lake Scenic Area天马湖景区

Tianma Lake Scenic Area sits about 9 km north of Funing District, Qinhuangdao, centered on a reservoir with a horse-shaped landmark sculpture on its shore. It historically offered boating, fishing and simple water rides for family outings. As of recent reports (2025), the site has been temporarily closed with no confirmed reopening date, so visitors should verify its status before planning a trip.

Qipanshan (Chessboard Mountain) Scenic Area棋盘山景区

Qipanshan Scenic Area, covering about 1.2 sq km, lies in Qianshangzhuang Village, Liutianzhuang Township, Lulong County — the site of the ancient Guzhu state, where legend says the noble brothers Boyi and Shuqi lived in seclusion. The park takes its name from a large boulder atop the peak resembling a chessboard, with a folk tale claiming its stone "pieces" can be moved but never removed. It also features terraced flower fields and a roughly 600-mu fruit-picking orchard, and served as a model anti-Japanese resistance base during WWII, blending natural scenery, legend and revolutionary history.

Baozigou Grape Valley鲍子沟景区

Baozigou is a grape-growing village and scenic area in Hebo Town, Lulong County, surrounded by hills near the 40th parallel — good grape-growing latitude that has earned it the nickname "Turpan by the Bohai Sea." Grapes have been cultivated here for over 300 years, and roughly 1,500 mu of farmland now grow Rose Honey and other varieties such as Sunshine Rose and Kyoho. Since 2002 the village has combined grape farming with rural tourism, offering fruit picking, rock formations, hilltop views, and lakeside fishing. It is a recognized national agri-tourism demonstration site, popular for family fruit-picking trips.

Liuhe Valley柳河溪谷

Liuhe Valley lies north of Liuhe Beishan Village in Liutianjiazhuang Town, Lulong County, next to the Jidong Anti-Japanese War Memorial Hall. From 1943-1945 it served as the office of the Jidong 12th District Committee, making it a stronghold village of the Jidong anti-Japanese base area — hence its local reputation for "longevity, revolutionary history, and greenery." Surrounded by hills and water, the site has forest cabins and villas, and combines organic farming, fruit picking, fishing, and patriotic-education tourism. It is often visited together with nearby Biixia Manor and other Lulong countryside scenic spots.

Liuhe Manor Winery柳河山庄

Liuhe Manor is a winery-resort in Fengjiashan Village, Liutianjiazhuang Town, Lulong County, built by a local wine company. It combines wine-grape vineyards, a winemaking facility, and manor-style grounds with leisure lodging in wooden cabins, making it a representative "wine plus tourism" destination near Qinhuangdao. Visitors can tour the vineyards and winery, learn about the winemaking process, and relax on the grounds. It suits wine enthusiasts, business groups, and couples looking for a quiet countryside getaway.
