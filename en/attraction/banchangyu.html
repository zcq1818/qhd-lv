About

Banchangyu Great Wall Scenic Area sits about 29 km from downtown Qinhuangdao in a village that served as a logistics base for Ming-dynasty Great Wall construction. Excavations in 2002 uncovered roughly 600 Ming-era brick kilns used to fire wall bricks — the largest such find in China — along with the world's only known fossil of a hyena species, now displayed on site. A steep, undulating stretch of wall known as the "inverted Great Wall" is a favorite subject for photographers, and the park also has a temple, a cave, a narrow rock passage and waterfalls. Autumn foliage against the old wall is a particular highlight. It suits Great Wall enthusiasts, photographers and visitors interested in Ming-era military history and archaeology.

Before you go

  • The Ming-dynasty brick kiln ruins here are the largest such find in China and have significant archaeological value — look out for signage or guided commentary.
  • The "Upside-Down Great Wall" (Daogua Changcheng) section has rough footing; it's great for photos but watch your step.
  • Fall foliage paired with the Great Wall makes for excellent photography, so autumn is the recommended season for photographers.
  • The scenic area includes several scattered sites — Tianran Temple, Lingxian Cave, and the Jiudaogang Waterfalls among them — so plan your route before you set out.

Tickets Tickets start around ¥50, though prices and combo packages vary by channel — some sources list ¥100 — so go with the price posted at the scenic area on the day of your visit. There's no consistent public information on discounts for seniors, children, or students, or on whether the brick-kiln exhibition and sightseeing shuttle are included, so confirm before buying.

Frequently asked

Is Banchangyu Great Wall the same as the Great Wall at Shanhaiguan?

No. Banchangyu is a separate section of the Ming Great Wall, known for its brick-kiln ruins, and is a different scenic area from Shanhaiguan — double-check your destination before you go.

How much are tickets?

Prices range from about ¥50 to ¥100 depending on the source, with no confirmed official rate — check with the scenic area or a booking platform before buying.

Is the "Upside-Down Great Wall" section easy to walk?

This section is fairly steep and rugged, so take care while climbing and avoid unsuitable footwear.
