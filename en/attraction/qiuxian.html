About

Located on the coast in Haigang District, this site is traditionally linked to Emperor Qin Shi Huang's legendary quest for the elixir of immortality — the story that gave Qinhuangdao its name. The park features a roughly 6-meter statue of the emperor, a shrine-style hall, a prayer plaza, and a Warring States-themed garden. It's a small, low-key seaside park good for a short history-themed visit or photos, and works well for families doing light historical sightseeing.

Before you go

  • The site is small and fits easily into a half-day itinerary while exploring downtown Qinhuangdao.
  • It's windy by the sea, so dress warmly in spring and autumn.
  • The statue of Qin Shi Huang is the main photo spot.
  • Worth visiting if you're curious where the name 'Qinhuangdao' comes from.
  • There's little shade in the park, so watch for sun exposure in summer.

Tickets Free (the general admission fee, previously about ¥30-45 depending on the source, has been dropped in recent years). Multiple sources say the site now has no general admission fee, though older material mentions ticket prices, half-price for children, and free entry for seniors and military. Whether individual facilities within the park are still charged separately could not be confirmed, so check the on-site notice or the official account before you go.

Hours 08:00-17:00, mainly open in peak season (April-October); hours for other months and the exact closing time should be confirmed in advance.

Frequently asked

Is there still an admission fee?

According to available information, the general admission fee has been dropped and the site is free to enter, but confirm through official channels or on site in case individual facilities are charged separately.

Is the name 'Qinhuangdao' connected to this site?

Yes — according to legend, Qin Shi Huang sent alchemists to sea from this spot in search of immortality, and the city's name, 'Qinhuangdao,' comes from that legend.

Is it good to bring kids?

Yes, the site is small with a statue and themed installations, making it good for a light, low-effort history lesson and photos.
