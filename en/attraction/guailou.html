About

Strange House Garden (Guailou Qiyuan) stands near Baihua Hill on Lianfeng Road in Beidaihe. It was rebuilt in 1991 based on a whimsical 1930s villa designed by an American landscape scholar who once lived in Beidaihe, with the park's name inscribed by cartoonist Hua Junwu. Covering about 999 square meters, the maze-like building is famous for its many doors and windows, hidden passages, tilted floors, and mirror rooms that create disorienting optical effects, each room built around a different fantastical theme. Because it's entirely indoors, it works well as a rainy-day option and is popular with families and children.

Before you go

  • The interior is maze-like, so keep a close eye on children to avoid getting separated.
  • Some rooms have slanted floors and strong mirror reflections — take care if you're prone to dizziness or balance issues.
  • Since it's entirely indoors, it's a good backup on rainy or overcast days.
  • A visit takes about 1-1.5 hours, and it pairs well with nearby Lianfeng Mountain Park on the same day.

Tickets Around ¥20 (sources vary between ¥15-20); prices differ slightly across platforms, so check the current price and any combo-ticket discounts on Ctrip or Meituan before buying.

Frequently asked

Why is it called the 'Strange House'?

The name comes from its maze of doors and windows, tangled layout, slanted floors, and mirror effects that create a disorienting sense of space.

Can I visit the Strange House Garden on a rainy day?

Yes — the attraction is entirely indoors and unaffected by weather, making it a popular rainy-day option.

What age is the Strange House Garden suitable for?

Suitable for most school-age children, though the interior is dim and complex, so parents should stay with kids throughout.
